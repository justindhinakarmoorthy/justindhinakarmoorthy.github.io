Dharapuram (SC) · By-election, 6 October 2026

In the Villages, TVK Fought AIADMK. In the Town, It Fought DMK.

Dharapuram is really two constituencies on a single ballot. In April 2026, the villages gave AIADMK 45%. The town gave it just 24% and put DMK in front. In the first election it faced, TVK fought two battles: it found its votes in different places. In the villages, it was strongest where AIADMK was weak, and in the town, it was strongest where DMK was weakest. So 6th October is TVK against AIADMK in the villages, and TVK vs DMK in the town.

April 2026 · 320 booths

Two Dharapurams

AIADMK won in April 2026 with 40.6% of the votes. It wasn't a majority: TVK and DMK together took more than 55% of the votes. The three-way split handed a village-strong AIADMK an edge.

The majority of the booths were in villages, 199 out of 320. This meant AIADMK won almost 7 out of 10 booths. In other words, it won almost everywhere in the villages.

In the villages, AIADMK was dominant. It took 45% of the vote and won 170 of the 199 village booths. DMK came second with 30%, and TVK was a distant third at 21%.

The town panchayats looked much the same. AIADMK got 41% and won 44 of the 61 booths.

The town was a different story. In Dharapuram's 60 town booths, AIADMK fell to third with just 24%. DMK led with 39%, and TVK was close behind at 33%. AIADMK won only 7 town booths, DMK won 29 and TVK won 24.

In fact, most of TVK's booth wins, 24 out of 31, came from the town.

So where did AIADMK win the seat? In the villages. It came out of the villages 18,663 votes ahead of DMK and added 3,747 more in the town panchayats. DMK won the town by 5,498, but that was nowhere near enough to close the gap.

In other words, DMK and TVK fought over the town, while AIADMK won the seat in the villages.

Where TVK found its votes

Two battles

Sort the village booths from TVK's weakest to its strongest. As TVK rises, AIADMK falls. DMK barely moves.

Do the same in the town and the picture flips. Where TVK was strongest, DMK was weakest. AIADMK barely moves.

One election can't show voters moving from one party to another. But it shows where each fight is: TVK against AIADMK in the villages, TVK against DMK in the town.

The base, the allies, the split

DMK's math

DMK lost in April, but it did not collapse. Even with a new party in the race, one in three voters still chose DMK. It came first or second almost everywhere, in the town and in the villages.

But that one in three was not DMK alone. In April, DMK stood with Congress, VCK, IUML, MDMK and the Left. Today Congress, VCK, IUML and MDMK are with the TVK government, and the CPI has its own candidate in Dharapuram.

On 6 October, DMK is fighting without most of the allies who helped build its April vote.

That makes the math harder. Even if DMK held every April vote, it would win only if AIADMK and TVK split the rest almost exactly in half. That is a coin landing on its edge, not a plan.

And holding every April vote is now the best case, not the starting point.

This is DMK's best case. It assumes DMK keeps all 63,336 of its April EVM votes, including the voters who came to it through Congress, VCK, IUML, MDMK and the Left, and that the only voters who change sides are Sathyabama's April voters who follow her to TVK.

In other words, DMK cannot count on the split. First it has to keep the voters who came to it through its allies. Then it has to grow. At 35%, it wins across a wide range of splits. That is a real path, not a lucky one.

6 October will test DMK's poll strategy. What it learns here will help it plan for future elections without its allies, in the areas where it relied on them.

Sathyabama's voters

TVK's path to victory

TVK came third in April, with 23% in its first election.

Now Sathyabama is its candidate. Every voter who follows her counts twice: one less for AIADMK, one more for TVK.

If just over 17,000 follow her, about one in five, TVK wins.

Winning DMK voters is harder. It would take more than half of them. For TVK, one AIADMK voter is worth two DMK voters.

And TVK has help. It is in government, and DMK's April allies are now on its side.

The risk: many voted for the Two Leaves, not for her, and AIADMK has a new candidate. So TVK's path runs through the villages.

Counting day · 9 October

What to watch

Three questions decide this by-election.

TVK

Can TVK win over the villages?

In April, TVK was a town party. It won 24 of its 31 booths in the town and got just 21% in the villages. But Sathyabama's voters are in the villages. If TVK is going to win, this is where it has to grow.

What to check
  • TVK's share in the village booths, compared with April's 21%.
  • Does it grow most where AIADMK was strongest? That would mean Sathyabama's voters followed her.
DMK

Can DMK hold its base?

In April, one in three voters chose DMK, in the town and in the villages. But that was with its allies. This time it stands alone.

What to check
  • DMK's share, compared with April's 32%.
  • Does its floor hold? In April it fell below 20% in only 11 booths.
  • Does it keep the town and its rural belt?
  • Where it slips, who gains: TVK or the CPI?
AIADMK

Can AIADMK hold its village fortress?

In April, the villages were AIADMK's fortress. It won 170 of 199 village booths and took 45% of the village vote. In the town, it came third. If the walls crack in the villages, AIADMK has nowhere else to make up the votes.

What to check
  • AIADMK's village share, compared with April's 45%.
  • How many of its 170 village booths does it keep?
  • Does it hold the town panchayats too, where it won 44 of 61 booths?

Compare vote shares, not raw votes. By-election turnout will probably be lower than April's 90%, so every party may lose votes even where it gains ground.

About the data

  • Results come from the ECI Form 20 for Dharapuram (AC 101), April 2026. Constituency totals include postal votes. Booth-level figures and the scenarios use EVM votes.
  • Each booth's area (village, town panchayat or Dharapuram town) comes from the polling-station list in the electoral roll.
  • Scenarios assume everyone else votes as in April and turnout does not change. In DMK's math, NTK, independents and NOTA hold about 4%, and DMK's extra votes come from them or from new voters.
